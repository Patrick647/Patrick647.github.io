abstract: |
  Objective: Docetaxel has a narrow therapeutic window and large inter-individual pharmacokinetic variability. Through a prospective study, this project aimed to determine the safety window of the steady-state area under the concentration–time curve (AUC) of docetaxel in breast cancer patients and the correlation between steady-state AUC and the occurrence of myelosuppression. Methods: A total of 101 patients with pathologically diagnosed stage I–III breast cancer receiving adjuvant or neoadjuvant chemotherapy from June 2019 to June 2021 were selected, and the steady-state AUC of docetaxel was measured, calculated and analyzed. Results: The reference range of the docetaxel safety window was a steady-state AUC of 1–1.6 mg·h·L⁻¹. Within this range, no severe myelosuppression was observed, and the incidence of grade I–II myelosuppression was 15.9%. When the reference range was 1.7–2.5 mg·h·L⁻¹, the incidence of myelosuppression was 21.4%, and the incidence of severe myelosuppression was 14.2%; when the reference range was 2.6–3.0 mg·h·L⁻¹, the incidence of myelosuppression was 66.7%. Among patients with a docetaxel steady-state AUC of 1–1.6 mg·h·L⁻¹, there was no significant difference in the incidence of myelosuppression between those who did and did not receive granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (P > 0.05). Conclusion: The reference range of the docetaxel safety window is a steady-state AUC of 1–1.6 mg·h·L⁻¹; within this reference range, prophylactic use of granulocyte colony-stimulating factor may be considered unnecessary for patients.

## Key Findings

- Established docetaxel safety window at steady-state AUC 1-1.6 mg·h·L⁻¹
- No severe myelosuppression (grade III-IV) observed within the safety window
- Inter-individual AUC variability exceeded 3-fold (CV% > 29%) despite BSA-based dosing
- Statistically significant difference in severe myelosuppression between AUC ≤1.6 and ≥1.7 mg·h·L⁻¹ groups (P = 0.017)
- No significant benefit of prophylactic G-CSF for patients within the safety window (P = 0.693)

## Significance

This prospective study provides evidence for personalized docetaxel dosing in breast cancer chemotherapy through therapeutic drug monitoring, potentially reducing unnecessary G-CSF prophylaxis and associated adverse effects while maintaining treatment efficacy.
